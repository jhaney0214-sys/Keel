# A free second opinion on your ALM model

*Jordan Haney, CFA, jhaney0214@gmail.com*

Your ALM model's answers go to ALCO, the board and the examiner, and most
credit unions have never seen them checked by a second, independent model
run on the same book. I am offering one or two credit unions that check,
free, in exchange for candid feedback.

## What you get

- **Your balance sheet in Keel**, an ALM, planning and liquidity model,
  tied line by line to your general ledger.
- **A side-by-side comparison** with your current model: NII by scenario
  for years one and two, NEV (or EVE) and the NEV ratio by scenario, and
  the opening balances. Each difference is marked within or outside a
  tolerance a reviewer would accept, and the ones outside come with where
  to look first: deposit betas, decay, prepayment or discount rates.
- **The full Keel report**: rate risk, the five-year plan, liquidity
  stress, capital, product profitability and the budget, all from one
  projection. It is yours to keep.

## What it takes from you

About two hours of staff time, and these files as of one quarter-end:

1. The loan, certificate, share, investment and borrowing detail files your
   core system exports (the columns are listed in START-HERE.md).
2. The general ledger trial balance on the same date.
3. Your current ALM report for that quarter, or just its NII and NEV
   tables.
4. Optionally, the key assumptions it ran on (betas, decay, prepayment
   speeds).

## Your data

Keel runs on one computer and sends nothing anywhere. It can run on your
own machine, with your staff at the keyboard, so the files never leave the
credit union. If you would rather send them, they are deleted when we
finish, and nothing identifying the credit union is ever published or
shared.

## How good is Keel?

Every quarter Keel builds every federally insured credit union from NCUA's
public call report, forecasts three months, and is scored against the next
quarter's report. Over the four quarters to June 2026 its quarterly net
interest income came within a median 2.9% of what each credit union
reported, closer than a "last quarter repeats" forecast for 60% of credit
unions and for three quarters of those over $1 billion. The full record,
including where it does no better than that, is in VALIDATION.md. It has not yet been compared side by side
with a production ALM model on a credit union's own files, which is what
this offer is for.

## What I ask in return

Thirty minutes to go through the comparison together, and your honest view
of what was useful and what was not.
